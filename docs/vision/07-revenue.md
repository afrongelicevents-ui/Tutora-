# 07 · Revenue ideas and how the money would actually be collected

This document proposes **no prices**. It proposes **pricing experiments** and explains how money could flow. It is not legal or tax advice — speak to a Zimbabwean accountant and lawyer before charging anyone.

## Starting point (today)

- Families pay **tutors directly** by EcoCash, InnBucks, bank transfer or cash, and upload proof. The tutor confirms, and Tutora issues a numbered receipt that says **"Recorded on Tutora — Tutora does not handle money"** (D73).
- Tutora earns nothing yet. Every tutor is on `plan = 'free'`; the fields `plan` and `plan_until` are ready for later (D49).
- Running costs are about $0 on free tiers (see `10-technical-recommendation.md`).

## Ground rules

1. **Do not charge tutors just for registering** before Tutora brings them paying students.
2. **The free experience stays useful**: discovery, booking, paying the tutor, receipts, homework, the lesson room, in-app reminders.
3. Charge only for things that **cost Tutora money or clear extra work** (messaging, reports, partnership admin) or that **bring the tutor income** (commission on lessons Tutora found).
4. Prices must be **affordable in Zimbabwe** and tested with real families before being fixed.

## Three ways to collect money

| Option                                                      | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Good                                                                                                          | Hard                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Tutora collects, then pays tutors**                    | Parent pays Tutora through a payment gateway (e.g. **Paynow**, which supports EcoCash and OneMoney through its API [S18]). Tutora keeps its share and pays the tutor out.                                                                 | Commission is automatic; parents get one simple checkout                                                      | Tutora holds other people's money (trust/escrow duties, payout work, refunds, disputes); gateway fees on every payment; changes D73 and the Terms; Paynow needs a merchant account and a successful test before going live [S18] |
| **B. Tutor pays Tutora a monthly commission**               | Families keep paying tutors directly (no change). At month end Tutora sends each tutor a **commission statement** built from confirmed payments for lessons booked through Tutora; the tutor pays Tutora (EcoCash, bank, or Paynow link). | No change for families; Tutora never holds tutors' money; uses data that already exists (`payment`, `charge`) | Tutors may under-report or move students off-platform; collection effort; needs clear Terms                                                                                                                                      |
| **C. Parent pays Tutora directly for Tutora-only services** | For services Tutora itself provides (accountability plan, premium reports, Academic Partnership, SMS/WhatsApp reminders), the parent pays Tutora by Paynow / EcoCash / bank.                                                              | Simple and honest: Tutora is paid for its own service; no tutor money involved                                | Needs a payment flow and subscription tracking (`plan`, `plan_until`, extended to parents)                                                                                                                                       |

**Paynow facts checked (12 Nov 2026):** no sign-up or monthly fee; a **commission per transaction** (the merchant can absorb it, pass it on, or split it); large volumes can ask for a discount; mobile-money payments (EcoCash, OneMoney, Telecash) settle **one day after** payment; integrations start in test mode and go live after Paynow checks one successful test payment [S18]. The **percentage fee was not verified** (Paynow's fees page could not be read) — **price not verified, check before signing up**. Merchant registration documents were **not verified** — ask Paynow. EcoCash also runs its own developer API as an alternative (not costed).

**Recommended order:** start with **C** (Tutora-only services) because it keeps Tutora away from holding tutors' money. Add **B** (monthly commission statement) when Tutora is clearly bringing tutors new students. Consider **A** only later, with legal advice, if families ask for one checkout.

## Pricing experiments (what to test, how to measure)

No prices are set here. Each experiment uses real users on the **live site** (never fake data), small groups, and clear "this is a trial" wording.

| Experiment                               | What to test                                                                                                                      | How to measure                                                                                  | Inputs to use                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Willingness to pay for accountability | Offer the paid accountability plan to a small group of parents after 4 free weeks; test 2–3 price points chosen with local advice | % who start paying; % still paying after 2 months; check-in consistency of payers vs non-payers | Messaging cost per family (`06` formulas) so the price covers it |
| 2. Report value                          | Show one sample of a premium report (the child's own real data) and offer monthly reports                                         | Opens, downloads, conversions                                                                   | PDF cost ≈ $0 (made on request)                                  |
| 3. Commission tolerance                  | Ask tutors (interview + simple survey) which model they prefer: % of first months, flat per new student, or monthly statement     | Tutor sign-ups and retention after commission is announced; reported payment volume             | Tutor's own prices (`subject.price_cents`)                       |
| 4. Partnership demand                    | Offer the Academic Partnership to a few parents whose child's school has agreed                                                   | Number of consents signed, renewals at term end                                                 | Admin hours per partnership (track them)                         |
| 5. Payment method preference             | Offer Paynow (EcoCash/OneMoney) vs bank vs InnBucks for Tutora-only services                                                      | Completed payments per method; failed payments                                                  | Paynow per-transaction fee (verify first)                        |

**Affordability check:** for each paid service, write down (a) Tutora's real monthly cost to deliver it (messaging, admin time), (b) the gateway fee, and (c) what local families already pay for comparable services — then test prices above (a)+(b).

## Legal, tax and trust points (high level — not advice)

- **Holding money (option A)** may make Tutora responsible for client funds, refunds and disputes, and may bring payment-service rules. Get legal advice before doing it.
- **Tax:** charging fees means Tutora has taxable income and may have VAT and invoicing duties in Zimbabwe. Ask an accountant; keep proper invoices (receipt numbering logic already exists, D14/D53).
- **Terms and Privacy** (drafts, D78) say Tutora does not process payments. They must be updated before any payment flow changes.
- **Children:** paid features for children are bought by the **parent**, never by the child.
- **Trust:** keep the receipt wording honest about who received the money. Never show invented "savings" or "results guaranteed" claims.
- **Data protection licence:** charging does not change it, but Tutora likely needs a POTRAZ data-controller licence anyway (see `08`).
